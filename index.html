The residence

The water comes to the end of the garden.

Bayou Sound is one of the quiet streets of Bay Isles, the gated heart of Longboat Key. It is not a street you pass on the way to anywhere else. The house stands directly on the canal, with its own dock at the end of the garden, and from that dock the water runs out into Sarasota Bay and on to the Gulf of Mexico.

It is a house composed around that view. The rooms on the water side open toward it through tall glass, the terraces and balconies step out above it, and the light it throws back into the interior changes the house through the day, silver and cool in the morning, warm and golden by evening. Once you are inside, the street is gone. What remains is the canal, the mangroves on the far bank and the wide sky over the bay.

Few houses on the island hold their position as well. Lifted above its garden, set back behind palms and flowering planting, it has the presence of a private villa and the ease of a house that is genuinely lived in, with room for family, for guests who stay the season and for the boat that waits at the dock.

The architecture

Drawn by hand, built to the line.

The house began as a drawing. The architect's sketch set the proportions you see from the street today: a strong white volume raised above the garden, a band of clerestory windows running beneath the roof, and a single sculptural wall that turns the facade into something closer to a gallery than a front elevation. Nothing was added that the drawing did not ask for, and nothing it asked for was left out.

The materials are few and chosen to last in a coastal climate. Smooth white stucco, frameless glass on the balconies, hurricane impact-rated windows and doors throughout, and warm wooden garage doors that soften the white. Wide steps rise through a garden of palms, bird of paradise and bromeliads to the front door, so that arriving at the house already feels like arriving somewhere.

Three levels, each with a purpose. One for arriving, one for living, one for sleeping above it all.

The main level

One generous floor for the life of the house.

Living room, kitchen and dining room open into one another beneath high ceilings, with light falling from the clerestory above and the water filling the glass on the other side.

The kitchen is built around a long stone island where the family gathers before anyone sits down, with a wet bar and wine storage close at hand in the living room. The dining room takes a wing of its own, large enough for a table of eight and open to the view. This is a floor made for hosting, and it does so with ease: kitchen, terrace and pool are a few steps apart, and every evening ends with guests at the glass rail, watching the light go down over the canal.

From here the house steps outside. The terrace runs along the water side, and below it the heated pool sits inside its own screened garden room, sheltered, private and in use every month of the year.

The private rooms

A suite on the water, and rooms for everyone who comes to stay.

The primary suite occupies its own wing of the main level and looks straight out across the canal. It has the proportions of a fine hotel suite and the privacy only a house can give: a bedroom open to the view, a walk-in closet, and a bath in warm stone with a freestanding soaking tub, a long double vanity and a generous open shower.

A second bedroom with its own bath sits on the same floor, ideal for parents or guests who visit for the season. Upstairs, a gallery landing looks down into the living room and leads to two further bedrooms sharing a Jack and Jill bath. Four bedrooms in all, each one a room you would be glad to wake up in.

The garden level

Everything island life asks for, kept out of sight.

At garden level the house takes care of the practical side of living on the water. An oversized two-car garage, and storage enough for bicycles, golf clubs, paddleboards and everything the beach and the boat require, all kept well away from the living floors above.

At the back, beneath the balcony, a covered terrace opens into rooms that can become a private gym and spa, a few steps from the pool and from the dock. It is the part of the house many owners come to love first: a workout, a swim, and the boat ready at the end of the garden.

Bay Isles, Longboat Key

Between the bay and the Gulf.

Longboat Key is a slender barrier island between Sarasota Bay and the Gulf of Mexico, and Bay Isles is its most private address.

A gated community at the center of the island, Bay Isles is a place of lush, quiet streets, deeded access to the Gulf beach and neighbors who know each other by name. Unfamiliar cars do not appear here, and that is precisely the point.

The beach is about five minutes away by car, the Longboat Key Club with its golf, tennis and marina about the same. The restaurants and boutiques of St. Armands Circle are some twenty minutes across the bridges, downtown Sarasota twenty five, and Sarasota Bradenton airport around half an hour. And from the dock at the end of the garden, the canal opens onto the bay, the passes and the open water of the Gulf.
